=== 5. SPEED VS COST VS TEST-INTEGRITY TRADE-OFFS (options only, not a recommendation) ===
Option A — route recon/mechanical phases (explorer-shaped, docs-shaped) to haiku, keep implementation/gate roles on sonnet, escalate only judgment-heavy surfaces (contract/data-model/auth/tenancy, or "what the diff omits") to opus, per the existing role pins and team-lead.md's own escalation-trigger list. Buys: matches the one clean completed-task cost data point (F5/haiku, $0.2635, cheapest per-turn measured) and the existing doctrine's own downgrade rule. Costs: not validated as an A/B — a cheaper tier could in principle need more turns to reach Done-when, which the superlinear-cost finding says would erode the saving; not measured either way.
Option B — enforce a hard per-phase call/turn budget derived from a spec-computed blast-radius (git grep count) rather than a blanket ~70-call ceiling, with the workflow script itself failing the phase early (WIP-commit + StructuredOutput blocked) rather than letting it run to 140-157 calls. Buys: directly addresses the largest single cost driver measured (superlinear per-turn growth; F2/F4 both breached budget ~2x and their back-halves cost 2.5-2.7x their opening rate) and the $6.47 100%-waste F1 failure was exactly this failure mode. Costs: requires the spec format changes in §4 to exist first (the budget can't be phase-sized without the blast-radius field); a too-tight budget risks more false "blocked" returns and more re-dispatch overhead (not measured, a plausible risk only).
Option C — split the pipeline into multiple Workflow-script invocations at every CONFLICT point (§3: test-plan freeze, key-decision escalation, qa exclusivity) versus attempting to force a single continuous script and papering over the human-STOP with a timeout/poll loop. Buys: matches the doctrine's actual human-in-the-loop requirements exactly (test-plan freeze is explicitly "hard block," key decisions explicitly "the human owns every key decision") and avoids inventing a suspend/resume mechanism the brief's primitive list doesn't document. Costs: more orchestration complexity in the lead layer (which script to launch next, carrying state/run-log across invocations) versus a single script's simplicity; whether Workflow's "resume by cached prefix" could instead support a true suspend was NOT established (see §6) — if it can, Option C's script-splitting overhead may be unnecessary, but this is unverified.
Option D — parallelize checker/tester/qa more aggressively (running phases in parallel() rather than the strict serial-after-all-implementation ordering the one fully-observed run used) versus keeping them strictly serial. Buys: potential wall-clock speed. Costs: the one fully observed run (wf_4eb1edf7-db8) shows real damage from strict serialization too — checker round 1 was largely a false-positive grep, and the real regressions surfaced only in round 2 "when there was no budget left to fix them" (per the spec-gatherer's sourced quote of the team's own feedback doc) — and tester/checker/qa independently hit and separately debugged the identical e2e ENV-DEFECT (docker/port conflict) because nothing shared "this environment is broken" state between them, costing "several dozen calls" combined per that same sourced quote. Neither the serial nor a more-parallel alternative was measured here as a controlled comparison; both the cost of strict serialization (found) and the cost of environment non-exclusivity under looser parallelism (a structural risk per qa.md's ENV-LOCK rationale, confirmed directly) are real, opposing pulls.
