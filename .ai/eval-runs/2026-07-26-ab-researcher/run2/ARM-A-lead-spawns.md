# ARM A — External-tool dependency survey of `skills/`

**Question answered:** which external tools does the sailes framework depend on, where is each referenced,
what version constraints are stated, and what does the framework say to do when a tool is absent.

**Scope:** `skills/` only. 80 files, verified: `find skills -type f | wc -l` = 80.
**Role of this document:** synthesis of three handed-over recon reports, reconciled and spot-verified
against the repository. See §4 (Method) for what was reconciled versus what was re-checked at source.

**Reading the table:** every row carries at least one repo-relative path. A claim without a path was
dropped, not softened. `Confidence` is stated only where the claim is not certain; unmarked rows were
either read directly by me or spot-verified by grep against the cited file.

## 4. Method

**Constraint.** I was forbidden to spawn any subagent, and did not. Every verification below was run by me directly with `Read`, `Grep`, and shell (`find`, `wc`, `grep`, `sed`, `ls`).

**Effort split.** Roughly 60% of the work went to *ingesting and reconciling* — the three reports total 2,407 lines and ~133K tokens, and all three exceeded the read cap, so each had to be paged (Explorer 3 alone needed four passes and one retry after a chunk blew the token limit). Roughly 40% went to *verifying at source*: 11 shell/grep batches covering the file inventory, every stated version pin, the disputed stack claims, all 23 bootstrap line counts, both hook scripts read in full, and a 43-name sweep for external tools that all three reports might have missed (result: none — `Storybook` was the only probe with zero hits, correctly).

**Was the handed-over slicing lossy?** **No gaps and no overlaps.** `find skills -type f | wc -l` returns 80; the slices were 23 (`sailes-bootstrap`) + 17 (`sailes-test`, `sailes-design`, `sailes-async`) + 40 (everything else) = 80, and the per-directory counts reconcile exactly (bootstrap 23; test 5 + design 7 + async 5 = 17; the remaining twelve directories plus `skills/README.md` = 40). Every file is claimed by exactly one explorer.

**But the slicing did cause damage of a different kind.** Because each explorer could only see its own slice, both aggregate claims made across slice boundaries were wrong or misleading (C1, C2), and the two most valuable findings — the TanStack/Next.js baseline contradiction (C4) and the `agents/` vs `~/.claude/agents/` drift (C3) — are *only visible* when the slices are read together, and neither explorer flagged them. Cross-slice contradiction detection is work the slicing structurally cannot do; it has to happen at reconciliation. That is the load-bearing observation about this architecture.

**What I did not do.** I did not re-read all 80 files. I re-read the two hook scripts in full and verified ~35 targeted anchors spread across all three slices, weighting the checks toward version constraints, absence behaviours, and any claim two reports touched from different directions. The medium-confidence rows in §2 mark where I relied on an explorer's transcription without an independent read.
